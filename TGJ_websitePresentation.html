If it weren't for the legacy of our predecessors, I wouldn't be able to wear these masks today.

The first mask is a standard type, covering only the eyes, the kind most people imagine when they hear the word "mask." Nowadays, it's often used to create a mysterious atmosphere at masquerade balls and Halloween. Its history dates back to the 16th century, originally used in Europe for character creation in improvisational mask theater. Gradually, it came to be used to conceal identity at parties and spread to people outside of theatrical performances.

The second mask is called a Hannya, and it's used in Noh, a type of Japanese musical theater. Its history dates back to the 14th century, when it was a form of entertainment for Japanese aristocrats. Hannya masks are meticulously handcrafted by Japanese artisans, symbolizing traditional Japanese craftsmanship.

The last mask is my original design, made using animal fossils. This means that fossils are something that can only be found after hundreds, even thousands, of people have come together. Furthermore, fossils are symbols of not only human history, but also the history of the entire planet. They are the bones of animals that lived tens of thousands of years ago, which have petrified over vast periods of time. If even one piece of history were missing, this mask could not have been made.

In this way, even my single assignment is filled with the history of humanity, living things, and the Earth.
